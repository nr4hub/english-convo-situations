1

The situation

You need to call or visit the APIIT front office/admissions counter to ask about your degree program details, or to help a friend find out how to join the next intake. This is a more formal conversation, so polite phrasing matters.

Tip: Start formal calls with a greeting + your reason for calling. End with a thank-you. Use “could/would” instead of “can” for a more polite tone.
2

Key phrases

Polite, formal phrases for asking about your course or a new intake.

  • Good morning, I'd like some information about the Cybersecurity degree program, please.සුබ උදෑසනක්, මට Cybersecurity ඩිග්‍රි වැඩසටහන ගැන විස්තර ටිකක් ඕන.
  • Could you tell me the duration and structure of the course?පාඨමාලාවේ කාලසීමාව සහ ව්‍යුහය කියන්න පුළුවන්ද?
  • I'm currently a student here. Could you check my module results for me?මම දැනට මෙතන ශිෂ්‍යයෙක්. මගේ module ප්‍රතිඵල පරීක්ෂා කරලා දෙන්න පුළුවන්ද?
  • When does the next intake start, and how do I apply?ලබන intake එක ආරම්භ වෙන්නේ කවදද, අයදුම් කරන්නේ කොහොමද?
  • What documents do I need to bring for registration?ලියාපදිංචියට මොනවගේ ලේඛන ගේන්න ඕනද?
  • Thank you so much for your help.ඔයාගේ උදව්වට ගොඩක් ස්තූතියි.
3

Sample conversation

A full conversation at the APIIT admissions counter.

At the APIIT admissions counter
You
Good morning. I'd like some information about the BSc in Cybersecurity program, please.සුබ උදෑසනක්. මට BSc Cybersecurity වැඩසටහන ගැන විස්තර ටිකක් ඕන.
Officer
Good morning! Sure, are you a current student or are you asking for someone else?සුබ උදෑසනක්! ඔව්, ඔයා දැනට ශිෂ්‍යයෙක්ද, නැත්නම් වෙන කෙනෙක් වෙනුවෙන් අහන්නේද?
You
I'm a current student, but my friend is interested in joining the next intake. Could you tell me when it starts?මම දැනට ශිෂ්‍යයෙක්, ඒත් මගේ යාළුවෙක් ලබන intake එකට එකතු වෙන්න කැමතියි. ඒක ආරම්භ වෙන්නේ කවදද කියන්න පුළුවන්ද?
Officer
The next intake begins in January. Applications open about six weeks before that.ලබන intake එක ජනවාරි මාසේ ආරම්භ වෙනවා. අයදුම්පත් ඊට සති හයකට කලින් විවෘත වෙනවා.
You
That's great, thank you. What documents will they need for registration?ඒක හොඳයි, ස්තූතියි. ලියාපදිංචිය සඳහා මොනවගේ ලේඛන ඕනද?
Officer
A copy of their O/L and A/L results, NIC, and two passport photos should be enough to get started.ඔවුන්ගේ O/L, A/L ප්‍රතිඵල පිටපතක්, ජාතික හැඳුනුම්පත, සහ passport ඡායාරූප දෙකක් ඇති ආරම්භ කරන්න.
You
Perfect. And while I'm here — could you also check my current module results?සුපිරි. මම මෙතන ඉන්නකොටම — මගේ දැනට තියෙන module ප්‍රතිඵලත් පරීක්ෂා කරලා දෙන්න පුළුවන්ද?
Officer
Of course, may I have your student ID number?ඇත්තෙන්ම, ඔයාගේ student ID අංකය දෙන්න පුළුවන්ද?
You
Yes, here it is. Thank you so much for your help today.ඔව්, මෙන්න. අද ඔයාගේ උදව්වට ගොඩක් ස්තූතියි.

5

Grammar focus

Polite request forms for formal, front-office conversations.

Polite modal verbs — could / wouldCore structure

‘Could’ and ‘would’ soften a request and sound more polite than ‘can’, which is important in formal settings like an admissions office.

  • Could you tell me the course duration?පාඨමාලාවේ කාලසීමාව කියන්න පුළුවන්ද?
  • Would it be possible to check my results?මගේ ප්‍රතිඵල check කරන්න පුළුවන්ද?
Future with 'will' / present simple for schedulesGrammar point

Fixed, scheduled events (like an intake date) often use the present simple even though they refer to the future.

  • The next intake begins in January.ලබන intake එක ජනවාරි මාසේ ආරම්භ වෙනවා.
  • Applications open next month.අයදුම්පත් ලබන මාසේ විවෘත වෙනවා.
Indirect questionsFormal style

Turning a direct question into an indirect one (‘Could you tell me when...’) sounds more polite than asking directly.

  • Could you tell me when it starts?ඒක ආරම්භ වෙන්නේ කවදද කියන්න පුළුවන්ද?
  • (Not: When does it start? in this formal context)
6

Common mistakes

Errors that make formal requests sound less polite or unclear.

Common mistakeCorrect versionWhy
Can you tell me when intake start?Could you tell me when the intake starts?Use ‘could’ for politeness and keep the verb in simple present, no inversion, inside indirect questions.
I want know about course.I'd like to know about the course.Use ‘I'd like to’ and don't drop the article ‘the’.
What is documents I need?What documents do I need?Use ‘do’ to form the question, not ‘is’.
Please to check my result.Please check my results. / Could you check my results, please?Don't use ‘to’ after ‘please’; also make ‘results’ plural.
